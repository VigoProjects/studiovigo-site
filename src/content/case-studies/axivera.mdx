**Tools:** Figma, Claude

<Text title="Summary">

Vigo ran discovery, prototyping and pathfinder user testing for Axivera, a clinical trials platform. Jobs to be Done interviews with trial coordinators, data managers and operational leads produced a prioritised feature set. The tested prototype then grew into a design system covering every core module, ready for Axivera’s engineers to build from.

</Text>

<Text title="Discovery and problem definition">

Working closely with Axivera’s team, Vigo set out to build a shared understanding of who the platform was for and what problems it had to solve. The work ran as three connected workstreams:

-   **User definition.** Mapping the people likely to use the platform, from trial coordinators and data managers to senior operational leads.
    
-   **Jobs to be Done.** Structured interviews and workshops to uncover the functional, emotional and social jobs users needed the platform to do.
    
-   **Feature mapping.** Turning those jobs into specific product capabilities, so every feature traces back to a real user need.
    

The result was a prioritised feature set based on evidence rather than assumption. That reduces the risk in every later phase and gives Axivera confidence in where the product is heading.

</Text>

<Text title="Prototyping">

With the problem clearly defined, Vigo moved quickly into prototyping. Building on established UI libraries meant core screens and flows could be designed without making every component from scratch.

Vigo was deliberately selective. Rather than detailing the whole product, the prototype focused on the screens that best showed what made the platform different, the moments where it most clearly improved on how trials are run today. Stakeholders and test participants spent their time on the parts of the product that mattered most.

Fewer screens done properly communicate more than a light pass across everything.

</Text>

<Figure src="/assets/screenshot-2026-03-27-at-14.58.59.png" alt="" gradient="coral-purple" />

<Text title="User testing and validation">

Vigo recruited a group of pathfinder users who closely matched the primary personas and tested the prototype with them. Each session looked at three things:

-   **Comprehension.** Did users understand what the platform was, what it offered and how it worked?
    
-   **Usability.** Could they move through the core flows without friction or confusion?
    
-   **Value.** Did the prototype convincingly improve on how they work now?
    

Their feedback went straight into a refined version of the prototype, fixing pain points before wider feature work began.

</Text>

<Text title="Iteration and feature build-out">

Once the core proposition had been validated, Vigo extended the design to new product modules using the same principles and components. Each new module inherited the rigour of the earlier phases, which kept quality high while the work moved quickly.

The end product is a coherent design system covering all core modules, built to extend to future features and ready to hand to Axivera’s engineering team.

</Text>

<Figure src="/assets/screenshot-2026-03-27-at-14.59.33.png" alt="" gradient="coral-purple" />

<Text title="What the design system is used for">

A tested prototype and design system at this stage are useful well beyond the engineering handover. They typically support:

-   **Investor and board conversations.** An interactive prototype shows the product far more convincingly than slides.
    
-   **Pilots with early customers.** It’s a low-risk way to gauge interest and gather feedback before full launch.
    
-   **Hiring and onboarding.** New designers and developers get up to speed faster with a documented system.
    
-   **Regulatory documentation.** Design work grounded in evidenced user needs can support submissions that require proof of user-centred design.

</Text>

<Text title="Tools and methods">

-   **Figma** for wireflows, prototyping and the design system.
    
-   **Jobs to be Done** as the framework for identifying user needs and prioritising features.
    
-   **Claude** to speed up research synthesis, turn qualitative findings into structured outputs and support documentation.
